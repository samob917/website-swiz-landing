# Voice

Taken from Ab's sent email to programs, not invented. Everything quoted below
is verbatim from real threads.

## How he actually writes

> "I wanted to ask if you need any help building your fellowship call, block
> and clinic schedules. At Scheduling Wizard, we build resident, fellow and
> faculty schedules for departments at Mass General, LA General, Yale, and
> other hospitals. We handle the build as a service, so you don't have to
> learn new software; we build the schedules for you."

> "Most programs we talk to are piecing together coverage, requests, and
> requirements by hand. We take your rules and requests and give you back the
> finished schedules in your preferred format on excel or through a live
> viewing link we design for you."

> "we'd love to partner with your fellowship and take the scheduling burden
> off your plate."

> "This includes us building your schedules and any major redrafts of the
> schedules that may come up as needed."

## What that tells us

**Short sentences.** Rarely more than two clauses. The blog runs 40-word
sentences with three subordinate clauses.

**Plain verbs.** "We build." "We handle." "We take your rules and requests
and give you back the finished schedules." No engine, no optimization, no
constraints, no mathematically anything.

**He names the problem operationally, not legally.** "Piecing together
coverage, requests, and requirements by hand." Not "compliance exposure", not
"accreditation risk". The reader already knows it is hard; he describes the
work, not the consequences.

**He almost never mentions ACGME.** The cold email does not say it once. The
blog says it in nearly every paragraph. He leads with taking the burden off
someone's plate.

**No fear.** Nothing in any thread about citations, reviews, violations or
accreditation being at risk. Not once.

**Proof is a short list of names.** "Mass General, LA General, Yale, and
other hospitals." No percentages, no "trusted by", no count of departments.

**Warm but brief.** "No worries at all!" "That sounds great!" "Happy to set
up another call, send more information, or answer any questions." Friendly,
never chummy, never salesy.

**Direct asks.** "Would you have 20 minutes for a brief call next week?"

**No em dashes anywhere.**

## Two things the website is missing

**The live viewing link.** "in your preferred format on excel or through a
live viewing link we design for you." The site only ever says Excel. He
offers a viewing link he builds for them, and that is a real differentiator
nowhere on the site.

**Redrafts are part of the price, not an extra.** "This includes us building
your schedules and any major redrafts of the schedules that may come up as
needed." The site treats redrafts as a capability. He sells them as included.

## The rewrite test

Before and after, same claim:

| Blog as written | In his voice |
|---|---|
| "Manual scheduling in Excel creates a high risk of ACGME violations, as complex rules can mask non-compliance" | "Most programs are piecing together coverage, requests and requirements by hand." |
| "Our optimization engine mathematically enforces every constraint" | "We take your rules and requests and give you back the finished schedules." |
| "Seamlessly integrates with your existing viewing platform" | "In your preferred format, on Excel or through a live viewing link we design for you." |
| "Eliminates hundreds of hours of chief resident work" | "We take the scheduling burden off your plate." |

## Rules for editing the posts

1. Sentences under 25 words. Break anything longer.
2. Say what we do before saying what is hard.
3. ACGME only where the paragraph is actually about a rule.
4. Never mention citations, reviews or accreditation consequences.
5. No engine, optimization, constraints, mathematically, guaranteed,
   seamless, robust, leverage.
6. Name hospitals rather than counting departments.
7. No em dashes.
